Linked List Sorting

Two classic sorting algorithms, both implemented by rewiring existing nodes' pointers instead of allocating anything new. The merge sort splits the list into its naturally-occurring sorted chunks, then repeatedly merges them back together two at a time. The quicksort picks a pivot, threads every node into one of three smaller lists (less than, equal to, greater than the pivot) by relinking pointers as it walks, recursively sorts the outer two, and splices all three back together. Same underlying skill in both: real pointer surgery on a linked structure, no shortcuts.

Escape the Labyrinth

Navigates a maze of cells linked by raw north/south/east/west pointers, following a string of moves step by step and failing the moment a move points into a wall (nullptr). Tracks which required items have been picked up along the way, succeeding only once every item is collected before the path runs out. Includes a personalized twist — the maze itself is generated from your name, so escaping it means finding your own unique way out.

Karplus-Strong Synthesis (MUSIC!)

A music-making toy built in C++: click lights on a grid to add notes to a looping beat, like a simple drum machine. Every note is generated live, not pre-recorded, using a classic trick called Karplus-Strong synthesis.

Each note is a short buffer representing a sound wave. “Plucking” it fills the buffer with a sharp burst of values. Then, on every playback, each sample gets replaced with the average of itself and its neighbor, scaled down slightly — repeated over and over, that smooths and fades the burst into something that sounds like a real plucked string.

Stack one of these per note, let clicks toggle them on and off, and mix together whatever's playing each tick — a real instrument built from basic math and careful memory management.

Redistricting

Recursively splits a population grid into equal-population districts, trying horizontal and vertical cuts at every position and backtracking until every resulting region matches the target population. A backtracking take on a real redistricting problem.

T9 Predictive Text

Given a sequence of phone keypad digits, backtracks through every letter combination each digit could represent, pruning branches early against a dictionary to only pursue valid prefixes, and returns every real word the sequence could spell.

Power Index (Voting Blocs)

Computes each voting bloc's power index by backtracking through every possible coalition and checking which blocs are the “critical” swing vote in each winning combination. Models a real political concept (the Banzhaf power index) used to analyze weighted voting systems like the Electoral College.
